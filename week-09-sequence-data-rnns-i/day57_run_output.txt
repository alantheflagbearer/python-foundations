3. LONG-RANGE SENSITIVITY -- does x_0 still reach h_T at long range?
======================================================================
Conv1DNet (Day 56) had a HARD structural cap: a kernel of size k literally cannot combine information more than k positions apart. An RNN has no such hard cap -- h_T depends on h_{T-1}, which depends on h_{T-2}, ... all the way back to x_0, by construction. But does that CHAIN carry a meaningful amount of signal at long range, or does it fade out? Measured directly via a finite-difference perturbation of x_0, not assumed.

T        |dh_T/dx_0| (finite diff)
5                         0.703337
10                        0.656980
20                        0.440420
40                        0.000024
80                        0.000000
160                       0.000000

Actual output from this lesson's run.

real, honest finding: sensitivity of the final hidden state to the VERY FIRST input starts at 0.703337 at T=5, is still 0.440420 at T=20 -- but by T=40 it has collapsed to 2.35e-05, and by T=160 it is exactly 0.0 (float64 has run out of precision to represent it at all). Unlike Conv1DNet, there is no hard cutoff distance beyond which x_0 provably cannot reach h_T -- the connection always exists structurally. But this measurement shows that connection collapsing in practice, not staying flat, well before backpropagation ever enters the picture -- a direct, forward-pass-only preview of Day 59's real topic: vanishing (or exploding) signal through repeated tanh and matrix-multiply steps.

======================================================================
4. WHAT'S STILL MISSING
======================================================================
Today's RNN has never been trained -- every weight is exactly what random initialization produced, and Section 3 already shows that even the FORWARD pass alone can let long-range signal fade. Turning this into something that actually learns the order-detection task requires a backward pass: propagating gradient not just through one layer, but back through EVERY timestep, reusing the SAME Wxh/Whh/bh at each one (so their gradients must be ACCUMULATED across all T steps, not computed once). That algorithm is called backpropagation through time (BPTT) -- Day 58 derives and gradient-checks it from scratch.
